Gia Lai Gong Festival and Wild Sunflower Week 2026: day-by-day schedule, how to get there, what to see

The Gong Festival, Wild Sunflower Week and Chu Dang Ya Volcano events run from 30 October to 8 November 2026 in Pleiku ward, Bien Ho commune and nearby areas, as part of National Tourism Year, Gia Lai 2026. Everything below comes from the Gia Lai People's Committee press conference of 20 October 2026, as reported in the press. One thing to say up front: this year the festival is national in scale. The plan to invite other countries was moved to 2027, for security reasons and scheduling difficulties, according to Vice Chair Nguyen Thi Thanh Lich at the press conference.

Scale

The festival brings together 1,487 artists, including 29 teams from Gia Lai province and artists from 10 other provinces and cities, among them Lam Dong, Dak Lak, Quang Ngai, Thanh Hoa, Hue and Da Nang. Participating peoples include the Bahnar, Jrai, H're, Cham Hroi and E De. The cultural space of the gong in the Central Highlands has been on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity since 2005, and Gia Lai is home to large Bahnar and Jrai communities, the main bearers of that heritage.

On visitor numbers, the province expects 100,000 to 150,000 visits for the festival, and 60,000 to 100,000 for Wild Sunflower Week. These are the organisers' forecasts, not measured figures, so treat them as a rough guide to how crowded it may get. In the first nine months of 2026, Gia Lai received about 13.5 million visits, up 25% year on year, according to provincial figures released at the press conference.

Day-by-day schedule

Bien Ho and Chu Dang Ya cluster: 30 October to 1 November

This cluster opens the festival. In Bien Ho commune and around Chu Dang Ya volcano, the programme includes the opening ceremony, the launch of the Ia Nueng village community tourism product, the Gia Lai City Trail run («Dream of the Great Forest»), a local products market and an exhibition on the cultural space of the Gia Lai gong.

On 31 October, two events stand out in Ia Gri village, Bien Ho commune. A music and folk instrument exchange takes place there, with clubs, bands and art troupes taking part. From 14:00 the same day, a ceremony celebrates the newly restored communal house (nha rong) in Ia Gri village. Ia Gri is also a place to see wild sunflowers at the foot of Chu Dang Ya volcano, a crater once listed by a British travel magazine among the ten most beautiful volcanic craters in the world.

The local products market and the gong cultural space exhibition run throughout 30 October to 1 November in Ia Gri village.

2 to 5 November: no published festival events

The press conference sources checked so far publish no festival events for these four days. The gap suits wild sunflower viewing, since the flowers open from late October, look their best in November and last into December. Anyone travelling in this window should recheck the provincial schedule before setting off, as the organisers may add activities.

Central Pleiku cluster: 6 to 8 November

On 6 November, a street festival parades through streets in Pleiku, Dien Hong and Hoi Phu wards, with artist troupes in procession.

The Gong Night show starts at 20:00 on 7 November at Dai Doan Ket Square, Pleiku ward, with a representative of UNESCO in Viet Nam attending. This is the only evening event with a published start time, so arrive early for a spot if you plan to watch it.

From 6 to 8 November, the Pleiku Museum grounds and Dai Doan Ket Square host traditional cultural exchanges. Artist troupes recreate living spaces and run hands-on demonstrations of gong performance, statue carving, weaving, basketry and rice pounding. The same three days bring Gia Lai food, tourism product displays, local farm produce and a tribute to Gia Lai coffee, with 30 to 40 stalls presenting OCOP products and quality coffee on Anh Hung Nup street, plus an exhibition of traditional cultural artefacts.

A photo and video contest about the festival series runs from October to the end of December 2026.

How to plan the trip

Plan by cluster rather than by single days. If you only have the first weekend, take the Bien Ho cluster from 30 October to 1 November, which combines the opening with sunflowers at the foot of Chu Dang Ya. If you can only come the second weekend, take 6 to 8 November, anchor on the night of 7 November, and spend the days on cultural exchanges and coffee stalls.
